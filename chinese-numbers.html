Chinese lucky & unlucky numbers: the complete guide

In Chinese, numbers are judged by what they sound like. That one fact explains missing 4th floors, record-breaking licence-plate auctions, wedding dates and why a price ending in 8 sells better.

By the 449444 Editorial Team · Updated September 2026

In one sentence: 8 (发 prosper), 6 (溜 smooth) and 9 (久 lasting) are lucky; 4 (死 death) is avoided; and combinations such as 520 (我爱你, I love you) and 168 (一路发, prosperity all the way) work like a secret vocabulary.

Number slang: the secret vocabulary

Chinese speakers chain digits into phrases, especially in messages, prices, dates and plates:

  • 520 我爱你 “I love you”. 20 May has become an unofficial Valentine's Day.
  • 1314 一生一世 “for a lifetime”, and 5201314 “I love you forever”, a favourite for gifts and red envelopes.
  • 168 一路发 “prosperity all the way”, and 518 我要发 “I will prosper”. Both are common in shop prices and phone numbers.
  • 94 就是 “exactly!” and 666 溜溜溜 “awesome” are internet slang.
  • 250 二百五 means “idiot”. Never price a gift at 250.
  • 14 要死 “will die” and 514 我要死 are why some towers skip floors 14 and 24 as well as 4.

The economics: superstition has a price

Number luck is not just folklore. It shows up in prices:

  • Housing. A study of about 117,000 Greater Vancouver house sales (2000–2005) found that in neighbourhoods with an above-average Chinese population, addresses ending in 4 sold about 2.2% cheaper and those ending in 8 about 2.5% dearer. On an average home that was roughly CA$8,000–10,000.
  • Floors. Many Asian towers skip the 4th floor, and some Hong Kong towers skip 40–49 entirely. Richmond Hill, Ontario banned house number 4 on new homes in 2013, and Vancouver ended non-sequential floor numbering in 2015.
  • Brands. OnePlus went from the 3T to the 5, Nokia never released a Series 4, and China Southern Airlines skips aircraft registrations ending in 4.
  • Telecom. SaskTel asked for the new area code 639 instead of 474 to avoid the negative associations of 4.
  • Plates and phones. Lucky licence plates and phone numbers regularly sell at auction for sums in the millions.
For businesses: if you sell to Chinese-speaking customers anywhere in the world, check your prices, SKUs, hotline numbers, unit numbers and launch dates. It is cheap insurance. Book a Number Audit.

Etiquette: gifts, red envelopes and dates

  • Red envelopes (红包): use even amounts with 6, 8 or 9 (for example 88, 168, 666, 888, 999). Avoid anything with 4.
  • Gifts: give in pairs (好事成双). Never give sets of four, and never give clocks (送钟 sounds like attending a funeral).
  • Weddings: dates with 8 or 9, or 520 (20 May), are popular. The 7th lunar month is usually avoided. Try our Lucky Date Picker.
  • Phone numbers: numbers ending in 8888 or 6666 carry premiums. Numbers ending in 4 or 14 are cheaper. Score yours.

Regional differences

Tetraphobia is strongest in mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore and Malaysia, and it is also present in Japan and Korea. Cantonese speakers sometimes treat 3 as positive (生, life), while Mandarin speakers may hear 散 (scatter). Younger, online generations increasingly reclaim unlucky numbers with humour, which is exactly the spirit of 449444.

FAQ

Why is 8 lucky in China?

八 (bā) sounds like 发 (fā), to prosper or get rich. The Beijing Olympics opened at 8:08 pm on 08/08/2008.

Is 4 always unlucky?

Traditionally yes, because of 死 (death). In modern slang 4 can stand for 是 (yes) or 事 (matters), as in 94 就是 and 44 事事.

Is 7 lucky or unlucky?

Mixed. It can sound like 起 (rise) or 气 (anger), and the 7th lunar month is Ghost Month. In the West, 7 is widely considered lucky.
